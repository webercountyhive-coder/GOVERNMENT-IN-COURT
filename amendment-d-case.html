Votes that didn't count: the Amendment D case (2024)

Six weeks after the Prop 4 ruling, the Legislature put Amendment D on the 2024 ballot. Courts voided it before the election because of how it was published and described to voters.

Courts: Third District; Utah Supreme Court2024 UT 40, No. 20240965Decided Sept. 25, 2024Status: amendment voided; appellate decision issued

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE · WHO
Utah Legislature · Third District Court · Utah Supreme Court
  • Who sued: League of Women Voters of Utah, Mormon Women for Ethical Government and others, in the same case as GC 008.
  • What was challenged: S.J.R. 401 (Amendment D), 2024 Fourth Special Session; primary sponsor Sen. Kirk Cullimore, floor sponsor Rep. Jordan Teuscher.
  • The vote in the Legislature: Passed Aug. 21, 2024: Senate 20-8-1, House 54-21-0.
  • The judges: Judge Dianna Gibson (Third District); all five Utah Supreme Court justices.

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

To change Utah's constitution, lawmakers must publish the change in a newspaper in every county for two months and describe it honestly on the ballot. The courts said Amendment D did neither, so votes on it were not counted. Voters later approved a change to that newspaper rule in 2026.

The short version

  • Sept. 12, 2024: Judge Gibson ruled Amendment D "is void and shall be given no effect." Ballots were already printed, so votes on it were not counted.
  • Sept. 25, 2024: the Utah Supreme Court unanimously agreed: "neither constitutional prerequisite was met with respect to Amendment D."
  • Oct. 24, 2024 opinion (Justice Hagen): "We simply cannot read the word 'newspaper' out of the Publication Clause."
  • What followed: 2026's Amendment A (H.J.R. 10) replaced the newspaper-in-every-county rule with publication "in a manner provided by statute." The AP called it passed Nov. 5, 2026 (KUER).

What court rulings and records confirm

  • ¶9: "posting a public notice on a government website does not meet our constitution's express requirements."
  • ¶79: "if the ballot title would mislead a reasonable voter as to what they are voting for or against, the proposed amendment has not been submitted to the voters as the constitution requires."
  • ¶96: "the Legislature did not submit the true amendment to voters."
  • Ballotpedia reports both the 2024 Amendment A and Amendment D were voided over the newspaper publication requirement.
