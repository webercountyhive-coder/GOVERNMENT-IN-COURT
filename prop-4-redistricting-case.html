Who draws the maps? League of Women Voters v. the Utah Legislature

Voters passed Prop 4 in 2018 to set rules for drawing congressional districts. The Legislature replaced it. Courts have since ruled the repeal unconstitutional and ordered a new map, and the fight continues before the Utah Supreme Court.

Court: Third District; Utah Supreme CourtNo. 220901712Filed March 17, 2022Map 1 in use; panel-law challenge awaiting ruling

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE · WHO
Utah Legislature · Lieutenant Governor · Third District Court · Utah Supreme Court
  • Who is suing: League of Women Voters of Utah, Mormon Women for Ethical Government and six voters.
  • Who is being sued: The Utah State Legislature, its redistricting committee, Sen. Scott Sandall, Rep. Mike Schultz, Sen. J. Stuart Adams, and Lt. Gov. Deidre Henderson (official capacities).
  • Lawyers: Plaintiffs: Zimmerman Booher, Parr Brown, Campaign Legal Center. Legislature: Office of Legislative Research and General Counsel, Consovoy McCarthy. Lt. Gov.: Attorney General's Office.
  • The judges: Dianna Gibson until February 2026; then a three-judge panel of Catherine Conklin, Derek Williams and Roger Griffin.

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

Every 10 years, the lines for congressional districts get redrawn. In 2018, voters passed Prop 4 to set fair-drawing rules. Lawmakers later replaced it with their own law. Voters' groups sued. The Utah Supreme Court said lawmakers can't freely undo a voter initiative that reforms government, and a judge later picked a new map. The case is still going, now over a new law that moved it to a three-judge court.

The short version

  • July 11, 2024: the Utah Supreme Court ruled 5-0 that "government-reform initiatives are constitutionally protected from unfettered legislative amendment, repeal, or replacement."
  • Aug. 25, 2025: Judge Gibson ruled the repeal of Prop 4 unconstitutional and blocked the 2021 map.
  • Nov. 10, 2025: she rejected the Legislature's "Map C," finding it "was drawn with the purpose to favor Republicans," and adopted the plaintiffs' Map 1, which was used in 2026.
  • 2026: a new law (H.B. 392) moved the case to a three-judge panel. The plaintiffs argue that panel is unconstitutional; the Utah Supreme Court heard arguments Oct. 5, 2026, with no ruling yet.

What court rulings and records confirm

  • Judge Gibson, Aug. 25, 2025: "The nature of the violation lies in the Legislature's refusal to respect the people's exercise" (KUER).
  • Judge Gibson, Nov. 10, 2025: "In short, Map C does not comply with Utah law" (KPCW/KUER).
  • The Feb. 20, 2026 Supreme Court order said the appeal was "improper and we lack jurisdiction" (KUER).
  • Map 1, with a Democratic-leaning district in northern Salt Lake County, was used for 2026 (KUER; KSL).
  • The GOP-led initiative to repeal Prop 4 did not qualify for the 2026 ballot after signature withdrawals (Ballotpedia).
