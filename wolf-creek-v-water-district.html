CASE FILE · GOVERNMENT IN COURT · OGDEN VALLEY · GC 006

Water before building: Wolf Creek Resort Holdings v. the Wolf Creek water district

Developers in Eden say the local water and sewer district blocked their projects by refusing water service. The district says it has one culinary well and is protecting current customers.

Court: Second District (Ogden)Civil No. 250903485Filed May 5, 2025Status: no ruling found

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE · WHO
Eden, Ogden Valley · Wolf Creek Water and Sewer Improvement District · Second District Court
  • Who is suing: Wolf Creek Resort Holdings, et al. Shane Dunleavy is named as one of the owners (KSL).
  • Who is being sued: The Wolf Creek Water and Sewer Improvement District, et al.
  • Lawyers: For the developers' related company EVO: Parr Brown and Bruce R. Baird PLLC. Letters for the district came from Cohne Kinghorn.
  • What they want: At least $2.5 million for water shares and at least $17 million in lost development, at least $19.5 million total (KSL).
  • Planned projects: 331 homes, 292 condos and townhomes, a hotel and 60,000 square feet of commercial space (KSL).

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

To build houses, you need water for them. In Eden, a small local district runs the water. Developers say the district wouldn't give them water service, so they couldn't build, and they want money for that. The district says it only has one well for drinking water and has to protect the people already using it. A judge hasn't decided.

The short version

  • Filed May 5, 2025, in Second District Court, Civil No. 250903485 (KSL; Weber County Board of Adjustment packet).
  • The claim: the developers say the district improperly refused to secure water rights for their projects. Dunleavy: the district is "weaponizing their control of water to block development."
  • The district: it has one culinary source, the Warm Springs well, which "has greatly limited our options." It moved to dismiss June 13, 2025, saying the parties' contracts control.
  • Other figures: letters in a county board packet describe $54.5 million two ways: the district's lawyer ties it to this case, and the developers' letter describes two cases together. The district's March 10, 2026 letter to customers says developers are suing for "more than $45M." None of these is a court-confirmed current demand.

Claims no court has decided

These are what a party says in its filings. They are not findings.

  • The developers allege the district improperly required water shares and blocked development. No court has decided this.
  • Dollar figures ($19.5 million, $45 million, $54.5 million) come from news reports and letters, not from any court ruling.

What the district told its customers (March 10, 2026)

The district's board sent customers a letter on March 10, 2026. It is the district's own account, not a court finding. It says:

  • Developers have brought three lawsuits against the district and are also suing Eden Waterworks Company and the State Engineer. A fourth suit challenges the State Engineer's rejection of a developer well (EC5).
  • One suit says the district didn't give enough water credit for Wolf Creek Irrigation Company shares; a second similar suit is by a different developer; a third says some district spring water rights are no longer valid.
  • The district serves 1,344 homes; developers want more than 700 new connections inside the district and 400 more outside it.
  • The district says it has spent about $12 million since 2020 on water supply, and is "currently spending around $30 to $40K per month" on legal and hydrology work.
  • To pay for it, customers' bills include a $25 a month "legal defense fee", after a public hearing in September 2025, starting in November 2025.

Because the letter describes several separate suits, this page focuses on Case No. 250903485 and lists the others as related.
