A rare water denial near the lake: Mango-Spiral Jetty v. the State Engineer

The State Engineer denied most of a Promontory ranch's groundwater requests, citing a "concern of monopolization of a limited resource." The landowner sued, pointing to nearby applications that were approved.

Court: First District (Box Elder)Case No. 260100003Filed Jan. 8, 2026Status: no ruling found

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE · WHO
Promontory peninsula, Box Elder County · Utah Division of Water Rights · First District Court (Brigham City)
  • Who is suing: Mango-Spiral Jetty, LLC. Its registered agent is Matthew Garff, co-owner of the Ken Garff Automotive Group (Salt Lake Tribune).
  • Who is being sued: State Engineer Teresa Wilhelmsen and the Division of Water Rights.
  • The judge: Brandon Maynard, First District Court (summons).
  • Lawyers: Sen. Brady Brammer and his firm, Brammer Ranck (Salt Lake Tribune). The firm declined to comment.
  • What it is about: 1,260 acre-feet of groundwater for mining and ranching on a 5,300-acre ranch less than 2 miles from the shoreline.

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

In Utah you need the state's permission to pump groundwater. A big ranch near the Great Salt Lake asked for a lot of water. The state said no to most of it, worried that one owner would control too much of a small supply. The ranch says the state approved similar requests nearby, so the denial was unfair. A judge hasn't decided yet.

The short version

  • The request: 1,260 acre-feet: 300 for mining and the rest for ranching (Salt Lake Tribune). A lithium company, Waterleaf Phase 1, wrote that it intends to lease the water (Dec. 5, 2025 letter).
  • The decision: The State Engineer denied all but one application, approving 240 acre-feet for irrigation. She cited a "concern of monopolization of a limited resource" and an area whose hydrology is "poorly understood."
  • The lawsuit (Jan. 8, 2026): alleges the decision "acted contrary to statute, ignored substantial and significant evidence" and was arbitrary, and that nearby applications "mere minutes away" were approved.
  • Keller Cattle: According to the Salt Lake Tribune, the suit lists water right numbers belonging to Keller Cattle Corporation, linked to Speaker Mike Schultz, but does not name him. The Hive has not read the full petition to confirm this.

What each side says

QuestionMango-Spiral Jetty (petition, as quoted)State Engineer / Division of Water Rights
Was the denial fair?The engineer "failed to adequately support its decision" and acted arbitrarily.A spokesperson said the state is "confident" its decision was fair, consistent and appropriate (Salt Lake Tribune).
Monopoly concernThe concern is speculative because 1,260 acre-feet is "only a fraction" of available water.Six back-to-back applications "raises a concern of monopolization of a limited resource."
Nearby approvalsApplications "mere minutes away" were approved despite similar protests.Remaining water in the area is "limited" and the hydrology "poorly understood."

What court rulings and records confirm

  • The case is First District Court, Box Elder County, No. 260100003, Judge Brandon Maynard (summons posted by the Division of Water Rights; the Hive read the summons page only, not the full petition).
  • The State Engineer approved 240 acre-feet for irrigation and denied the rest (Salt Lake Tribune).
  • Keller Cattle's application for more than 550 acre-feet was approved in July 2024 (Salt Lake Tribune).

Claims no court has decided

These are what a party says in its filings. They are not findings.

  • Mango alleges the State Engineer applied different standards to its applications than to nearby ones. No court has decided this.
