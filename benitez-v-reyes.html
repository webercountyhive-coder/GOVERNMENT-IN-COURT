CASE FILE · GOVERNMENT IN COURT · UTAH ATTORNEY GENERAL'S OFFICE · GC 007

The employees' suit: Benitez et al. v. Reyes et al.

Employees of Rubicon and its sister companies, publicly linked to the 2023 human trafficking case that was later dismissed, sued the former attorney general and three members of his office.

Court: Third District, per KSL (caption not seen)Filed Nov. 18, 202538 plaintiffsStatus: no ruling found

Posted Nov. 6, 2026

WHERE · WHO
Utah Attorney General's Office (former officials) · Rubicon, Scandia and Smart Rain employees
  • Who is suing: 38 employees of Rubicon, Scandia and Smart Rain, "including company presidents, vice presidents, and a CEO" (KSL/Deseret). Dario Benitez is named as a plaintiff by one outlet.
  • Who is being sued: Former Attorney General Sean D. Reyes, former chief investigator Leo Lucey, investigator Michael Jeter and prosecutor Kaytlin Beckett.
  • Which court: KSL reports a Third District (state court) suit, separate from the owners' federal case. The plaintiffs' press release said U.S. District Court. The Rubicon lost-evidence complaint cites Third District No. 250909230. The Hive has not seen the filed caption.
  • Related: Part of the same events as GC 001 (lost evidence) and the Larsens' federal suit.

In plain words (explain it like I'm 10)

When the state charged Rubicon's owners in 2023, the employees' names and jobs got pulled into the news too. The charges were later dropped. Now 38 of those workers are suing the former attorney general and three of his staff, saying the investigation hurt their reputations and careers. No judge has ruled.

The short version

  • Filed Nov. 18, 2025 (Business Wire).
  • Claims: negligence, vicarious liability, violation of due process, unreasonable search and violation of open courts rules. They seek "lost wages, lost compensation and emotional distress" and punitive damages (KSL/Deseret).
  • Their words: employees were "publicly linked to manufactured criminal allegations that were later dismissed." Benitez: "Every person has the right to be judged on facts — not headlines."
  • A Jan. 30, 2026 motion to dismiss was filed by the AG's counsel, according to the Rubicon complaint. The Hive could not find the motion or any ruling.
